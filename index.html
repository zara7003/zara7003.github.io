Secure Money Manager ● Android app
Built for my own finance · v1.0 · GPL-3.0 · source download

My bank texts me every time money moves, and I wasn't willing to hand a finance app access to my accounts. So I built one that reads those messages on the phone itself and turns them into a proper multi-currency ledger — QAR, INR and USD kept separate, never averaged into one made-up number.

  • Requests no internet permission at all — Android itself refuses the app a network connection, so nothing can be uploaded
  • Reads payment SMS and notifications only from senders you approve, one at a time; everything else is discarded unopened
  • Encrypted database, biometric or passphrase unlock, exports to JSON, CSV and XLSX
⤓ Download the project (.zip) How I build these →

Shared as the Android Studio project itself — Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, GPL-3.0. It has been written and reviewed but not compiled by me, because I don't have an Android toolchain on my server: open the smm folder in Android Studio, let Gradle sync, then Build → Build APK(s). Read the README first, and try it before trusting it with real records.

How these get built
01 · THE TRIGGER
A need, usually my own

A physio routine I had to run at home. Payment messages I refused to keep inside someone else's finance app. A file I wasn't willing to upload to a stranger's server. Every tool here started as a small, personal annoyance.

02 · THE RULE
If it can run locally, it does

Whatever can happen inside your browser, happens inside your browser — no upload, no account, no analytics. Where a tool needs to touch your machine, it asks first and shows you exactly what it is asking for.

03 · THE METHOD
Vibe coded, with AI

I build these together with AI. I decide what should exist, what it must never do, and what "finished" means — then iterate until it works. AI writes a great deal of the code; the judgement and the responsibility are mine.

04 · THE HONEST BIT
I'm not a software developer

I lead teams and projects for a living: business development, tenders, contracts, delivery. I use the resources available to me to get the job done, and AI is one of them. You're looking at the output — not a claim about my job title.

More tools and solutions will appear whenever I run into the next need. Everything is free to use, and most of it never leaves your device.
